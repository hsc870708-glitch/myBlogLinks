  <h1 style="font-size:32px;line-height:1.35;margin-bottom:14px;color:#111;">Why LG Electronics (066570) Rose While the KOSPI Crashed: Robots, AI Infrastructure, and a Market Re-rating</h1>
  <div style="background:#eef6fb;border-left:6px solid #2f6f9f;padding:18px 20px;margin:18px 0 24px 0;border-radius:10px;">
    <p style="margin:0 0 8px 0;font-size:17px;"><strong>Reference point</strong>: South Korea market close on May 15, 2026</p>
    <p style="margin:0 0 8px 0;font-size:17px;"><strong>Key fact</strong>: LG Electronics closed at KRW 240,500, up 10.83% on the day, and touched KRW 266,500 intraday, a new 52-week high. That happened on the same day the KOSPI fell 6.12%, which makes the move stand out even more. 1) 2)</p>
    <p style="margin:0;font-size:17px;"><strong>Simple reading</strong>: Investors are no longer treating LG Electronics as only a home appliance maker. The market is increasingly pricing it as a company with proven earnings plus optionality in robotics, physical AI, and AI data-center infrastructure. 3) 4) 5) 6)</p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:30px 0 12px;color:#17324d;">1. At a Glance</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The most important point is not just that the stock rose, but <strong>the kind of day on which it rose</strong>. On May 15, the KOSPI briefly climbed above the historic 8,000 level, then reversed sharply and closed at 7,493.18, down 6.12%, as profit taking, foreign selling, and concerns tied to Middle East tensions hit sentiment. A sell-side sidecar was triggered during the session. 2) On a day like that, recently hot names usually get sold hardest. LG Electronics did the opposite and still finished higher. 1) That tells us this was more than a casual momentum spike.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The price action itself was strong enough to support that view. LG Electronics rose 18.00% on May 12, 3.52% on May 13, 13.38% on May 14, and another 10.83% on May 15. Based on those closing prices, the stock gained about 30% in four trading days. 1) Even on May 15, after opening at KRW 256,000 and swinging between KRW 233,000 and KRW 266,500, it still managed to preserve a double-digit gain into the close. 1) In plain language, there was profit taking, but buying demand was stronger.</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:30px 0 12px;color:#17324d;">2. Why the Stock Moved</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The first driver was <strong>robotics and physical AI expectations</strong>. After its first-quarter earnings call, LG Electronics said it had discussed cooperation with Nvidia in robotics, AI data centers, and mobility. The company did not frame this as a vague talking point. It said the discussions included broad cooperation across the robotics ecosystem and building a base for order growth and revenue conversion in data-center cooling. 4) That matters because investors are starting to see LG Electronics as a hardware enabler of AI, not just a seller of TVs and washing machines.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">Broker commentary reinforced that narrative. A Hana Securities note carried by Newspim on May 14 raised the target price to KRW 230,000 and highlighted several elements together: an actuator mass-production system for humanoid robots this year, an accelerated schedule for CLOi-related proof of concept work, rising data-center cooling orders, and talks with Nvidia related to physical AI, AI data centers, and mobility. 6) For a global reader, the point is straightforward: the market is reacting not only to buzzwords, but to the idea that LG Electronics is building the parts and systems needed to monetize those themes.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">Capital flows also support the story. According to Newsis, from May 4 to May 12 foreign investors bought 1,640,840 shares of LG Electronics, worth about KRW 267.2 billion. The same article described LG Electronics as one of the key Korean names benefiting from foreign buying in robotics-linked stocks. 7) A lot of rallies begin with headlines, but only the more durable ones start to pull in real institutional money. That is one reason the move has been taken seriously.</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:30px 0 12px;color:#17324d;">3. What the Earnings and News Flow Really Say</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">This rally is not built on imagination alone. LG Electronics reported first-quarter revenue of KRW 23.7272 trillion and operating profit of KRW 1.6737 trillion on April 29. The company said first-quarter revenue was the highest in its history for that quarter, and combined quarterly revenue from its home appliance and vehicle solution divisions exceeded KRW 10 trillion for the first time. It also reported KRW 6.5 trillion in B2B revenue and KRW 640 billion in subscription revenue. 3) That is a very important backdrop. Investors are more willing to pay for future optionality when the current business already looks solid.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The AI infrastructure angle is also grounded in actual business activity. In March, LG Electronics announced an agreement tied to an AI data-center project in Wanju, where it plans to supply industrial HVAC solutions including large chillers, CRAH equipment, and CDU liquid-cooling systems. The project is a 20MW AI data center with total investment of KRW 280 billion through 2029. 5) Then, in late April, the company said on its earnings call that data-center-related orders in 2025 had grown roughly threefold year over year, and that its chiller business could reach its KRW 1 trillion 2027 revenue target earlier than expected. 4) That is why the market is willing to attach an AI infrastructure label to the stock.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">Put differently, investors are now combining three pieces into one valuation story. First, first-quarter earnings proved the core business still has profit power. 3) Second, Nvidia-linked cooperation gives credibility to the robotics and physical AI angle. 4) Third, data-center cooling is turning from concept into visible order opportunities. 5) 6) That combination is far more powerful than a one-dimensional theme trade.</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:30px 0 12px;color:#17324d;">4. Sector and Market Context</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The broader Korean market in mid-May had been powered by AI enthusiasm and strong earnings, but May 15 showed how fragile that momentum could be once profit taking hit. 2) What makes LG Electronics interesting is that it held up on exactly that kind of stress test. 1) That suggests money inside the market may be rotating toward companies seen as beneficiaries of the AI buildout beyond semiconductors themselves, especially firms that combine large-cap liquidity, real earnings, and a new growth story.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">LG Electronics has also become more complicated in a good way. It used to be read mainly through consumer demand, TV cycles, and FX sensitivity. Now investors are also watching vehicle components, HVAC, subscription revenue, robotics parts, and home robots. 3) 4) 5) That changes what matters. The next questions are no longer only about appliance sell-through. They are about whether actuator production begins on schedule, whether CLOi proof-of-concept work advances as expected, and whether AI data-center cooling turns into meaningful sales. That is what a market re-rating looks like in practice.</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:30px 0 12px;color:#17324d;">5. What to Watch Next</h2>
  <div style="background:#fff8e8;border:1px solid #e7d2a1;border-radius:10px;padding:18px 20px;margin:10px 0 20px 0;">
    <p style="margin:0 0 10px 0;"><strong>First</strong>, watch whether robotics milestones arrive on time. Expectations have moved faster, so delays in PoC progress or actuator rollout could quickly trigger profit taking. 4) 6)</p>
    <p style="margin:0 0 10px 0;"><strong>Second</strong>, monitor whether AI data-center cooling becomes visible in orders and revenue, not just in presentations and interviews. 4) 5)</p>
    <p style="margin:0 0 10px 0;"><strong>Third</strong>, the second quarter matters. If the core business keeps generating healthy earnings, the market will be more willing to sustain a premium for new businesses. 3)</p>
    <p style="margin:0;"><strong>Fourth</strong>, market volatility still matters. A stock can show relative strength, but if the broader tape becomes disorderly again, even the strongest names can get dragged around. 2)</p>
  </div>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The bottom line is that LG Electronics is being treated less as a slow-growth appliance name and more as a company with a stable earnings base plus credible exposure to robotics and AI infrastructure. That does not remove risk. The stock has moved very fast, and the market will eventually demand harder evidence. But for now, the message from price action is clear: investors believe this is no longer just yesterday’s appliance company.</p>
