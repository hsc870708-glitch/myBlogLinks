  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Why the Stock Moved</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">For international readers, the key point is that SK hynix did not fall because a major earnings miss or negative guidance suddenly appeared on Friday. South Korea’s market had become extremely concentrated in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix after a dramatic semiconductor-led rally, and when sentiment turned, those were the positions investors rushed to cut. Weekly Donga reported that foreigners sold KRW 9.6892 trillion worth of SK hynix over the last five trading days, while retail investors absorbed KRW 10.7847 trillion. On Friday alone, foreign investors were the decisive force behind the KOSPI slump. 1) 4)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">This distinction is important. A strong business can still post a weak session when positioning becomes crowded and fast money starts locking in gains. SK hynix had closed at KRW 1.976 million on May 13, putting the symbolic “2 million won” level within reach. After such a steep run, the stock became one of the easiest liquidity sources in a market shock. In plain terms, investors were not suddenly saying the company’s AI-memory story was broken; they were saying they wanted to reduce exposure to the market’s most overowned winner. 4)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What the Fundamentals Still Say</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The underlying business story remains powerful. On April 23, SK hynix reported first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 52.5763 trillion and operating profit of KRW 37.6103 trillion, both record highs. The company said strong AI infrastructure spending continued to support demand for high-value products such as HBM, high-capacity server DRAM modules and enterprise SSDs. It also said capital spending would rise meaningfully this year, centered on the M15X ramp-up, Yongin cluster preparation and EUV equipment procurement. That is not the profile of a company losing its cycle; it is the profile of a company trying to expand fast enough to keep up with demand. 5)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">A Yonhap report released on May 15 added more color. According to the report, the United States accounted for 64.7% of SK hynix’s first-quarter revenue, Nvidia contributed KRW 7.7 trillion, and another big-tech customer generated sales in the KRW 6 trillion range. That tells global investors something simple but important: SK hynix is no longer just a memory maker benefiting from a cyclical rebound. It is increasingly being valued as a critical supplier inside the North American AI data-center buildout. 6)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The roadmap also matters. The company has said it plans to apply its 1c-nanometer process to HBM4E, targeting sample shipments in the second half of 2026 and mass production in 2027. Markets care about this because they are not only paying for current HBM3E profits; they are also paying for who keeps the lead in the next generation. Friday’s selloff hurt the share price, but it did not change that roadmap. 7)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Sector and Market Context</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The bigger market backdrop explains why the reaction was so violent. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix together had become unusually dominant in the KOSPI, amplifying both rallies and drawdowns. When the index broke above 8,000 intraday and then reversed, the selloff in those two semiconductor leaders fed back into the broader index decline. In other words, SK hynix was not only a stock inside the market; it had become one of the market’s main transmission channels for fear. 1) 4) 8)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Yet the medium-term demand backdrop still looks tight. Reuters reported last week that major global tech firms had approached SK hynix with unusual proposals, including funding new production lines or helping pay for expensive EUV tools, in an effort to secure memory supply. That is a strong signal that customer demand is still running ahead of available capacity. For beginners, this is the main takeaway: the stock can be weak in the short run even while the industry story stays strong. Short-term flow and long-term demand are not the same thing. 8)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What to Watch Next</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">There are three practical checkpoints from here. First, whether foreign selling in Korean semiconductor heavyweights begins to stabilize. Second, whether additional customer-validation news around HBM4E samples arrives in the second half. Third, whether capacity-expansion projects such as M15X and Yongin translate into credible supply growth without hurting financial discipline. If those pieces remain on track, Friday’s move may be remembered more as a positioning washout than as the start of a fundamental downtrend. 4) 5) 7) 8)</p>
