  <h1 style="font-size:30px; line-height:1.35; margin-bottom:14px; color:#111827;">Hanmi Semiconductor (042700): Why the Stock Fell 9.89% as a Q1 Earnings Shock Collided with HBM Expectations</h1>
  <div style="background:#eff6ff; border-left:6px solid #2563eb; padding:18px 20px; margin:18px 0 24px 0; border-radius:10px;">
    <p style="margin:0 0 10px 0;"><strong>At a glance</strong></p>
    <p style="margin:0;">Hanmi Semiconductor closed at KRW 369,000 on May 15, 2026, down 9.89% in a single session, after trading between KRW 406,000 and KRW 365,000. The broader backdrop was already weak, with the KOSPI dropping 6.12% to 7,493.18 and the KOSDAQ falling 5.14% to 1,129.82, but Hanmi’s decline was amplified by a sharp first-quarter earnings miss that forced investors to rethink how quickly the HBM equipment story will translate into numbers. 1) 2) 3) 4)</p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:28px 0 12px 0; color:#111827;">What moved the stock today</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The immediate trigger was earnings. Hanmi Semiconductor reported first-quarter consolidated revenue of KRW 50.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.456 billion, with operating profit down 87.9% year on year. Yonhap also reported that net profit fell 65.2% to KRW 19 billion. For an ordinary industrial stock, weak quarterly numbers can hurt. For a company that has been priced by the market as one of the cleanest HBM infrastructure beneficiaries, the reaction can be much harsher because investors are not just buying current profit, but a future growth curve. 2) 3) 5)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">That is the key point for international readers. Hanmi Semiconductor is not being judged only as a semiconductor equipment maker. It has been treated as a core lever on the AI memory build-out, especially through TC bonders used in HBM production. When a stock with that kind of premium narrative reports numbers far below market expectations, the question shifts from “is the industry still good?” to “is the current valuation still justified right now?” Korean media highlighted that the company’s actual first-quarter result came in far below market expectations that had been much higher, which helps explain why the selloff was so severe. 2) 5)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:28px 0 12px 0; color:#111827;">How the market is reading the mixed signal</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The story is not one-sided, though. On the same day, Hanmi said it is pushing to establish a U.S. local corporation and argued that TC bonder orders could become concentrated in the second quarter as HBM4 mass production ramps up, with momentum accelerating in the second half. In other words, management’s message was effectively: the first quarter was weak, but the order cycle may improve from here. The stock’s drop suggests that investors are not willing to simply accept that guidance on faith anymore. They now want visible contracts and real earnings recovery. 6)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">There is also a technology transition issue sitting underneath the stock. In April, Hanmi said it would release a prototype of its second-generation hybrid bonder within the year and work with customers on next-generation HBM production. But the same report noted that mass adoption of hybrid bonding is still expected around 2029 to 2030, partly because JEDEC is reviewing a relaxation of package height standards. That means investors are dealing with two timelines at once: near-term TC bonder monetization and longer-term hybrid bonder optionality. When quarterly earnings disappoint, both timelines get re-priced at the same time. 7)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">Past order flow matters as well. In January, SK hynix signed a KRW 9.65 billion TC bonder supply deal with Hanmi, widely interpreted as a sign that HBM4-related equipment investment was resuming. That was supportive. But investors are now asking whether those orders are merely restarting in a measured way or whether Hanmi can return to the kind of explosive order visibility the market had embedded into the share price. This is why the stock can fall hard even while the structural AI memory theme remains intact. 8) 9)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:28px 0 12px 0; color:#111827;">Sector and market backdrop</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">The broader Korean market made everything worse. On May 15, the KOSPI and KOSDAQ both sold off sharply. A Reuters-cited report described the move as a sharp reversal after a historic rally, with heavy reliance on AI-linked semiconductor names becoming a vulnerability once investors began taking profits. In that kind of tape, a stock carrying both a premium valuation and an earnings disappointment tends to be punished faster than the market. Hanmi Semiconductor was hit by both forces at once: a macro-style de-risking move across Korean equities and a company-specific reset in expectations. 3) 4) 10)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">This is also why it would be too simplistic to say that the market suddenly no longer believes in HBM. A better reading is that investors temporarily stopped paying for distant upside and demanded near-term evidence instead. The industry narrative around AI memory, HBM4 expansion and advanced packaging still exists, but on this particular day the market chose to focus on the gap between valuation and reported earnings. That distinction matters because it tells us what to watch next: proof, not just possibility. 2) 6) 7)</p>

  <div style="background:#fff7ed; border:1px solid #fdba74; padding:18px 20px; margin:24px 0; border-radius:10px;">
    <p style="margin:0 0 8px 0; font-weight:700; color:#9a3412;">Simple interpretation</p>
    <p style="margin:0;">The May 15 drop looks less like a clean rejection of the HBM theme and more like a valuation reset. The market marked the stock down because earnings were far weaker than expected, while future order recovery remains a promise that still needs to be proven. 2) 6)</p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:28px 0 12px 0; color:#111827;">What to watch next</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">First, investors will want to see whether the company’s claim about second-quarter TC bonder order concentration is backed by actual announcements and revenue recognition. Second, order patterns from SK hynix, Samsung Electronics and Micron matter because Hanmi’s valuation still depends heavily on HBM capacity expansion. Third, the competitive balance between current TC bonder leadership and future hybrid bonder positioning remains critical. Finally, because Korean equities have become increasingly sensitive to moves in AI-linked semiconductors, Hanmi should not be read in isolation from the broader tape. 4) 6) 7) 8) 10)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px 0;">So the cleanest summary is this: Hanmi Semiconductor remains an important HBM equipment name, but on May 15 the market cared much more about verified earnings than about the longer-term story. The next real test is whether the company can turn management commentary on second-quarter and second-half momentum into hard evidence. Until that happens, the stock is likely to trade not just on industry optimism, but on the market’s willingness to trust the timing of that recovery. 2) 6) 7)</p>
