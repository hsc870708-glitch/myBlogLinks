  <h1 style="font-size:30px; color:#1f3a5f; margin-bottom:14px;">South Korea Stock Market Close, June 3, 2026: Election Holiday, No KOSPI Session, and What Comes Next</h1>
  <p style="font-size:16px; color:#555;">There was no regular 3:30 p.m. closing data for the South Korean stock market on June 3, 2026. The Korea Exchange was closed for the nationwide local elections, meaning the KOSPI, KOSDAQ, ETFs, derivatives, and related financial markets did not trade that day. This report therefore does not invent daily movers or sector performance. Instead, it explains why the market was closed, what happened in the previous trading session on June 2, and what investors should watch when trading resumes on June 4.1)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">Why This Is a Holiday Report, Not a Normal Market Close</h2>
  <p>The most important rule in a market report is simple: do not describe prices that did not exist. June 3 was the day of South Korea’s ninth nationwide local elections, and the country’s main financial markets were closed. According to the Korea Exchange information reported by Korea JoongAng Daily, the closure applied not only to the main KOSPI market but also to the KOSDAQ, ETFs, derivatives, and commodity-related markets.1)</p>
  <p>That means there is no valid June 3 close for the KOSPI or KOSDAQ. It also means there were no official sector leaders, foreign net buying figures, institutional selling numbers, or daily stock rankings for the Korean equity market. For international readers, this is similar to a national market holiday: the price screen is quiet, but market-relevant information can still accumulate in the background.</p>
  <p>Election day itself was active across the country. Yonhap reported that voting began in local polling stations on the morning of June 3, including Jeju, where voters visited polling places from early in the day.5) The political event matters for the stock market mainly because it paused trading, and because policy expectations can sometimes affect theme stocks after the market reopens.</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">The Previous Session: KOSPI at a Record, KOSDAQ Under Pressure</h2>
  <p>The best reference point is the June 2 close. The benchmark KOSPI rose 13.11 points, or 0.15%, to end at 8,801.49, marking another record closing high. The tech-heavy KOSDAQ moved in the opposite direction, losing 24.00 points, or 2.29%, to finish at 1,026.03.2)</p>
  <p>This split is important. The KOSPI is more heavily influenced by large-cap exporters, financials, and major semiconductor names, while the KOSDAQ tends to include more growth and mid-small-cap stocks. A record KOSPI close alongside a sharp KOSDAQ decline suggests that the market was not broadly risk-on. Instead, investors were still willing to support large-cap index names, but they were more cautious toward recently strong growth stocks.</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">Investor Flows: Foreign Selling Met Strong Retail Buying</h2>
  <p>The June 2 session was volatile. AJU PRESS reported that the KOSPI opened strong, reached 8,933.62 shortly after the open, then fell as low as 8,503.48 as foreign selling intensified. By the close, the index had recovered enough to finish in positive territory. Foreign investors net sold about 8.0505 trillion won, while individuals bought about 8.1191 trillion won and institutions bought about 237 billion won.3)</p>
  <p>For beginners, the key point is not only that foreign investors sold. It is that the market absorbed that selling pressure and still ended higher. This usually means domestic liquidity, especially retail demand, was strong enough to cushion the fall. However, this can cut both ways. If foreign selling was merely short-term profit-taking after a fast rally, the index may stabilize quickly. If it was the start of a larger reduction in exposure, the next session could become more fragile.</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">U.S. AI Momentum and the Read-Through for Korean Semiconductors</h2>
  <p>While Korea was preparing for a market holiday, U.S. equities continued to show strength. On June 2, the S&amp;P 500 rose 0.1% to 7,609.78, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.4% to 51,307.79, and the Nasdaq Composite edged up to 27,093.90. AP reported that artificial-intelligence winners helped keep U.S. stocks near record levels, with Marvell Technology surging after comments from Nvidia’s CEO.4)</p>
  <p>This matters for Korea because the domestic market is highly sensitive to the global semiconductor cycle. Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, and other AI infrastructure-linked names often react to changes in U.S. chip sentiment. Still, investors should avoid reading the AI story as one-way bullish. The same AP report also noted that Alphabet weighed on the market after announcing a large capital-raising plan to help fund AI investments.4) In other words, AI demand remains a powerful theme, but the cost of building that future is also becoming a market question.</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">What to Watch on June 4</h2>
  <p>The first checkpoint is whether the KOSPI can hold above the psychologically important 8,800 area after its record close. A calm opening would suggest that investors are comfortable with the recent rally. A sharp reversal would suggest that the holiday gave traders time to reassess valuation and profit-taking risk.</p>
  <p>The second checkpoint is foreign flow. If foreign investors return as buyers, the market may interpret the June 2 selling as a temporary lock-in of gains. If they continue to sell, the burden on retail investors will grow. The third checkpoint is the KOSDAQ’s recovery. A rebound in growth stocks would show that risk appetite is broadening again, while further weakness would confirm that investors are rotating away from overheated areas.</p>
  <p>The fourth checkpoint is the semiconductor response to U.S. AI momentum. Korean chip names may benefit from the global AI narrative, but price reaction should be judged alongside volume and foreign flow. Finally, investors should watch policy-related themes after the local elections. Election-linked expectations can move quickly, but themes that are not backed by earnings or confirmed policy often reverse just as quickly.</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:24px; color:#253858; margin-top:28px;">Bottom Line</h2>
  <p>The June 3 South Korean stock market story is not about a hidden rally or a quiet selloff. It is about a full market holiday. The meaningful setup for the next session comes from four confirmed factors: the KOSPI’s record close on June 2, the KOSDAQ’s sharp pullback, heavy foreign profit-taking, and continued U.S. AI-led market strength. When Korea reopens on June 4, the first hour of trading should show whether investors still trust the large-cap rally or begin to demand a broader cooling period.</p>
