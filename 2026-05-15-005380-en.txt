  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Why the Stock Moved</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">To understand Hyundai Motor on Friday, it helps to start with what happened earlier in the week. On May 13, the shares crossed KRW 700,000 for the first time in history, with local media highlighting ongoing enthusiasm around Hyundai’s robotics angle. Once a stock breaks a symbolic level like that, short-term profit-taking often becomes more likely, especially if the overall market turns unstable. Friday looked like exactly that kind of session. 2) 3)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">This matters because not every down day tells the same story. A stock can fall because investors no longer believe the business outlook, or it can fall because too many gains were booked in a short period and traders decide to lock some of them in. Hyundai’s price action on May 15 fits the second pattern more closely. The company had become one of the market’s stronger non-semiconductor large caps, and when Korea’s equity market reversed sharply lower, even the recent winners were not spared. 1) 2)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What the Fundamentals Still Say</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The underlying financial picture is mixed but not broken. Hyundai Motor reported first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 45.9389 trillion, operating profit of KRW 2.5147 trillion and net profit of KRW 2.5849 trillion. Revenue hit a record first-quarter high, but operating profit fell 30.8% year on year. That combination is important: investors are looking at a company that is still generating large sales and preserving strategic momentum, but is also facing meaningful margin pressure. 4)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The details explain why the market has been willing to keep a constructive medium-term view. Hyundai said global wholesale sales fell 2.5% in the first quarter and that tariff effects amounted to KRW 860 billion. But the company also said hybrid sales hit a quarterly record and global electrified vehicle sales rose 14.2% year on year. In simple terms, Hyundai is not being treated as a company losing relevance. Instead, it is being judged on whether its shift toward hybrids and higher-value products can offset a tougher cost environment. 4)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">There was also a fresh product and technology angle just before Friday’s pullback. On May 14, Hyundai launched the new Grandeur and emphasized that it was the company’s first model to adopt its AAOS-based Pleos Connect infotainment platform, marking a more visible move toward software-defined vehicles. It also highlighted a next-generation hybrid system for the model. For investors, that reinforced the idea that Hyundai is not just a legacy automaker; it is trying to earn a higher valuation as a mobility and software platform story as well. 5) 6)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Sector and Market Context</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">That dual identity is exactly why Hyundai’s stock has become more interesting. It still belongs to the traditional auto sector, but parts of the market have begun trading it more like a future-mobility name, with value assigned to robotics, software, premium products and advanced hybrid systems. That creates upside when the story is working, but it also creates vulnerability when traders start asking whether the rerating ran too far too fast. Friday’s price action reflected that tension. 3) 6)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Monthly sales data also keep the market grounded. Hyundai said on May 4 that April global sales came in at 325,589 units, down 8.0% from a year earlier, citing supplier component shortages and waiting demand for new models. That is a useful reminder for beginners: even when a stock is rising on a future-facing narrative, investors still return to near-term sales, costs and margins. The valuation can stretch for a while, but real operating numbers still matter. 5)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What to Watch Next</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Three checkpoints matter from here. First, whether KRW 700,000 starts behaving like support after the breakout. Second, whether Hyundai’s hybrid and software-defined vehicle narrative translates into more visible sales and margin stabilization. Third, whether tariff and raw-material pressure begin to ease in the second quarter. If those pieces improve, Friday may look more like a pause after a surge than the start of a deeper reversal. 2) 4) 5) 6)</p>
