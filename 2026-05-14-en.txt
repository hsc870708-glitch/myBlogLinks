South Korea Market Close on May 14, 2026: KOSPI Ends at 7,981, Still Just Short of 8,000

South Korean equities finished higher on May 14, 2026, with risk appetite staying firm through the close. The KOSPI opened stronger on the back of a U.S. tech rally and briefly climbed to 7,991.04 during the session. It ultimately closed at 7,981.41, up 137.40 points or 1.75% from the previous day, marking another record closing high.

The tone was strong, but not without friction. Options-expiry supply and continued foreign selling capped the upside, keeping the benchmark just 19 points below 8,000. Even so, domestic buying from retail investors and institutions absorbed that pressure and kept the market in positive territory.

1. Snapshot of the Close

This was a broadly constructive session rather than a narrow rally led by only a few mega-caps. The KOSPI rose sharply, the KOSDAQ recovered from an intraday wobble to finish higher, and market breadth on the main board stayed decisively positive.

2. Indexes and Market Flow

The KOSPI closed at 7,981.41, while the KOSDAQ ended at 1,191.09, up 14.16 points or 1.20%. The KOSDAQ did slip into negative territory during the day, but buying returned late in the session. In the currency market, the won finished the daytime session at 1,491.0 per U.S. dollar at 3:30 p.m. Seoul time, up 0.4 won from the previous close.

Flows were the key story. On the KOSPI, retail investors bought a net 1.8377 trillion won and institutions bought 189.7 billion won, while foreigners sold a net 2.1674 trillion won. On the KOSDAQ, retail investors bought 91.3 billion won and institutions bought 58.9 billion won, while foreigners sold 148.4 billion won. Foreign investors had been net sellers on the KOSPI for six straight sessions since May 7, which remains an important overhang.

3. What Moved the Market

First, the overnight lead from U.S. equities mattered. On May 13 in the United States, the S&P 500 rose 0.58% and the Nasdaq gained 1.20%, both setting fresh record closes. That strength fed directly into sentiment around Korean semiconductor names.

Second, large-cap chip stocks did much of the visible lifting. Samsung Electronics rose 4.23% to 296,000 won and touched a fresh intraday high of 299,500 won. SK hynix also hit a new intraday high at 1.994 million won, though it later gave back gains and closed down 0.30% at 1.97 million won.

Third, the upside was checked by expiry-related positioning and headline risk. Options expiry brought additional supply into the market, while caution also picked up around comments tied to U.S.-China summit headlines. That helps explain why the KOSPI approached 8,000 but did not quite get there.

Fourth, domestic participation kept the tape resilient. On the KOSPI, 649 stocks advanced while 221 declined, showing that the day’s strength was spread across the market rather than concentrated in only one pocket.

4. Sector and Stock Highlights

Among sectors, construction rose 6.20%, entertainment and culture gained 5.94%, and medical and precision equipment added 5.65%. On the weaker side, electricity and gas fell 1.94%, transport equipment and parts dropped 1.15%, machinery and equipment slipped 0.76%, and transport and warehousing lost 0.45%.

Within the KOSPI heavyweights, Samsung Electronics, LG Energy Solution (+2.79%), Samsung C&T (+3.15%), Samsung Biologics (+2.77%), and Hanwha Aerospace (+1.56%) supported the advance. HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (-8.46%), Doosan Enerbility (-2.42%), and Kia (-0.78%) lagged.

On the KOSDAQ, Alteogen jumped 8.76% and held onto its position as the market-cap leader. EcoPro BM rose 6.04%, EcoPro gained 5.41%, Samchundang Pharm added 1.50%, and Leeno Industrial climbed 1.69%. Rainbow Robotics (-4.21%), Kolon TissueGene (-6.92%), and HLB (-0.56%) were among the weaker names.

5. What to Watch on May 15, 2026

The next session sets up around three questions. Can the KOSPI challenge 8,000 even if foreign investors remain sellers? Will the positive read-through from U.S. tech continue to support Korean semiconductors and growth stocks? And now that options expiry has passed, will intraday volatility calm down a bit?

The simplest takeaway is this: South Korea’s market kept pushing to new highs on May 14 because local buying was strong enough to offset foreign selling. The 8,000 level on the KOSPI is now the clearest near-term line to watch.
