  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:28px 0 12px;color:#111827;">What happened to the Korean stock market today?</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The simplest answer is that nothing traded in the regular cash session because the market was shut. There was no new closing level for KOSPI, no fresh KOSDAQ finish, no sector leaderboard, and no same-day foreign or institutional flow to interpret. On a holiday report, the real task shifts from “what rose or fell today” to “what could pile up in the background while trading is paused.” 1) 2)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">For beginners, a market holiday can feel like a blank page. In practice, it is often the opposite. Prices stop moving, but headlines, expectations, and worries do not. When trading resumes after a break, those delayed reactions can come back at once through the opening price, early volatility, and sector rotation. That is why a holiday briefing can still be useful even without a live market close.</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:24px;margin:28px 0 12px;color:#111827;">How should investors think about sectors and leading stocks on a holiday?</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Because there was no trading session, there is no honest way to rank semiconductors, autos, batteries, biotech, internet platforms, or financials by today’s market performance. But that does not mean sector thinking becomes irrelevant. In fact, the first session after a holiday often sharpens the differences between sectors because investors return to the screen with accumulated news and a need to express it quickly.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Export-heavy names may react first to currency moves and global rate expectations. Large technology stocks may reflect any change in demand sentiment around chips and AI-related supply chains. Domestic demand plays can react to consumer or policy headlines that emerged during the break. The key point is simple: on a holiday, do not ask which sector won today. Ask which sector is most exposed to the first batch of information once the market reopens.</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The same logic applies to representative stocks. Names such as Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix, Hyundai Motor, Kia, major battery stocks, large financials, and heavyweight internet shares may become the first scoreboard for broader sentiment on May 26. But any attempt to describe their May 25 regular-session price action as if it happened today would be inaccurate. The better approach is to watch whether their opening gaps reflect company-specific news or broad market mood.</p>
