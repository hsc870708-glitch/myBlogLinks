    <h2 style="font-size:23px;margin:0 0 12px 0;color:#111827;border-bottom:2px solid #e5e7eb;padding-bottom:8px;">1. What Moved the Stock Today</h2>
    <p style="margin:0 0 12px 0;">The easiest way to understand Samsung’s drop is to separate the trigger from the backdrop. The trigger was labor uncertainty. Reuters reported that Samsung Electronics’ South Korean union said it would stick to a planned strike starting next week even after management proposed resuming talks without conditions. Yonhap also reported that top executives visited the Pyeongtaek site and asked the union to return to talks, but the meeting only confirmed the gap between the two sides. 2) 4)</p>
    <p style="margin:0 0 12px 0;">For the market, that matters because Samsung is not just any blue chip. It is a systemically important semiconductor name, a major KOSPI heavyweight, and a core vehicle for global investors expressing the AI memory trade in Korea. When a stock with that profile is sitting near a 52-week high, even a temporary operational uncertainty can trigger an outsized reset in positioning. That is why the move looked violent: investors were not reassessing long-term demand first, they were reducing short-term exposure. 1) 2) 3)</p>
    <p style="margin:0;">So the price action was not saying “Samsung’s business has broken.” It was saying “the market suddenly dislikes uncertainty more than it likes the earnings story.” That distinction is important, especially for non-Korean readers trying to interpret a large one-day swing. 2) 3) 5)</p>
  </section>

Yonhap reported that the union has been preparing for an 18-day strike from May 21 to June 7. In a market already watching whether Korean equities had become overheated after a record run, that kind of headline was enough to push fast money into the exit. 2) 4)</p>

The Device Solutions division posted KRW 81.7 trillion in sales and KRW 53.7 trillion in operating profit, helped by higher memory prices and strong demand for AI-oriented products.     <h2 style="font-size:23px;margin:0 0 12px 0;color:#111827;border-bottom:2px solid #e5e7eb;padding-bottom:8px;">3. Sector and Market Context</h2>
    <p style="margin:0 0 12px 0;">Samsung’s fall did not happen in isolation. Yonhap said the KOSPI briefly broke above 8,000 for the first time ever before collapsing to 7,493.18, while foreigners dumped KRW 5.6 trillion worth of local shares and institutions sold another KRW 1.7 trillion. Analysts quoted by Yonhap said investors, especially foreigners, rushed to take profits in the same large-cap semiconductor and technology names that had powered the recent rally, including Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. 3)</p>
    <p style="margin:0 0 12px 0;">That matters because it changes the interpretation. If only Samsung had fallen while the rest of the market remained stable, the move would have looked like a company-specific warning. But with the KOSPI and KOSDAQ both hit hard, the day looks more like a broad market de-risking event in which Samsung became one of the main pressure points. Its sheer size and index weight made it a natural source of liquidity. 1) 3)</p>
    <p style="margin:0;">There was also a macro overlay. Yonhap pointed to lingering uncertainty around the Middle East crisis, while the Korean won weakened to around 1,500.8 per U.S. dollar at the 3:30 p.m. close. That combination of geopolitical nerves, profit-taking, and local labor risk created a very unfriendly setting for a crowded semiconductor long. 3)</p>
