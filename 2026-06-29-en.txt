<h1 style="font-size:28px;color:#111;margin:0 0 16px;">Korea Market Split: KOSPI Pressured by Chip Giants, KOSDAQ Lifted by Rotation on June 29, 2026</h1>
<p style="background:#f3f7ff;border-left:5px solid #2f6fed;padding:14px 16px;margin:16px 0;">South Korea’s equity market showed a clear split on June 29, 2026. Based on information available immediately after the 3:30 p.m. regular-session close, large-cap semiconductor names weighed on the KOSPI, while the KOSDAQ drew strong buying interest in biotech, rechargeable battery, robotics and IT materials stocks. Because final official closing figures were not yet consistently confirmed across reliable close reports at the time of writing, this article separates verified intraday figures from market interpretation after the close.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:22px;margin-top:28px;color:#17324d;">1. Quick Market Summary</h2>
<p>The day’s key message was not simply that “Korea fell” or “Korea rose.” It was a rotation market. The KOSPI remained under pressure because Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, the two most important index heavyweights, continued to face foreign selling and weaker global semiconductor sentiment. Yonhap reported that the KOSPI opened 0.91% lower at 8,334.28, fell further during the session, and saw significant foreign net selling in the main board. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix were both weaker, pulling down the benchmark index.1)</p>
<p>The KOSDAQ, however, moved in the opposite direction. Ajunews reported that by early afternoon the KOSDAQ had climbed more than 7% and recovered the 910 level, helped by strong moves in biotech, secondary battery, robotics and IT component stocks. A buy-side sidecar was triggered after KOSDAQ150 futures and the underlying index met the required surge conditions.2) For global readers, the most important point is that Korean equities are currently being driven by a very concentrated large-cap chip trade on one side and a policy-and-rotation trade in smaller growth sectors on the other.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:22px;margin-top:28px;color:#17324d;">2. Why Did the KOSPI Stay Heavy?</h2>
<p>The KOSPI’s weakness started at the opening bell. Chosun Biz reported that foreign investors had already sold more than KRW 1 trillion net in the KOSPI market shortly after the open, while retail and institutional investors were buyers.5) YTN later reported that the index briefly fell as low as the 8,127 area during the session.3) This was not just a broad panic move. It reflected the structure of the Korean market: when foreign investors reduce exposure to Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, the whole KOSPI can move sharply because those stocks carry an unusually large index weight.</p>
<p>The external backdrop also mattered. Yonhap noted that U.S. semiconductor names such as Nvidia, Micron and Broadcom had declined in the previous U.S. session, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropping 5.29%. The report also cited news that OpenAI was considering delaying its initial public offering, which weighed on technology sentiment more broadly.1) In a market where Korean memory-chip names had already rallied hard, that kind of U.S. tech pressure encouraged profit taking.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:22px;margin-top:28px;color:#17324d;">3. Why Was the KOSDAQ So Strong?</h2>
<p>The KOSDAQ’s strength came from a different story. Buying rotated toward biotech, battery materials, robotics and IT equipment names. Ajunews listed strong gains in stocks such as Alteogen, Ecopro, Ecopro BM, Rainbow Robotics, HLB and LigaChem Biosciences during the session.2) Yonhap also reported that expectations for KOSDAQ market-support measures ahead of the market’s 30th anniversary helped investor sentiment.1)</p>
<p>This rotation matters because June had been dominated by extreme volatility in the KOSPI. Yonhap reported that during the month, Korea’s main board saw 10 sidecar events and three circuit breakers, while the KOSPI200 volatility index surged to levels last seen around the global financial crisis era.4) When volatility becomes that intense, investors often search for areas that are less tied to the same crowded trade. Today, that meant KOSDAQ growth sectors rather than mega-cap semiconductors.</p>
<h2 style="font-size:22px;margin-top:28px;color:#17324d;">4. Major Stock and Sector Points</h2>
<p>Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix remained the central pressure points for the KOSPI. A Yonhap analysis published on June 28 said the two stocks accounted for 56.48% of KOSPI market capitalization as of the June 26 close.4) That explains why even a moderate percentage decline in the pair can feel much larger at the index level. On the other hand, selected large caps such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Energy Solution, Samsung Biologics and Doosan Enerbility showed strength during the session and helped offset some pressure.1)</p>
<p>For the KOSDAQ, the leadership came from sectors that investors often associate with policy support and a broader growth-stock recovery. The buy-side sidecar was a clear sign that buying was unusually strong, but it should not be interpreted as a guarantee that the move will continue. It simply tells us that demand was powerful enough during the session to trigger a market-stabilization mechanism.2)</p>
<h2 style="font-size:22px;margin-top:28px;color:#17324d;">5. What to Watch Next</h2>
<ul style="padding-left:20px;margin:12px 0;">
<li>Foreign selling in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix: a slowdown would ease pressure on the KOSPI.</li>
<li>U.S. semiconductor stocks and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index: Korea’s chip trade remains closely linked to Wall Street tech sentiment.</li>
<li>KOSDAQ follow-through: investors need to see whether today’s strength becomes a sustained rotation or only a short rebound.</li>
<li>FX and geopolitical risk: Middle East headlines and the won-dollar exchange rate can affect foreign flows.</li>
<li>July earnings season: the market will test whether expected strong results from major chipmakers can calm volatility.4)</li>
</ul>
<p style="background:#fff7e6;border-left:5px solid #f0a000;padding:14px 16px;margin-top:22px;">In short, June 29 was a split market. The KOSPI was held back by large semiconductor stocks and foreign selling, while the KOSDAQ benefited from rotation into biotech, batteries and other growth themes. The next session should be read through confirmed closing data, foreign flow updates and whether the KOSDAQ rally can survive beyond one strong day.</p>
