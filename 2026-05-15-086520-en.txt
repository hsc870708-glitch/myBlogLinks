  <h1 style="font-size:30px; margin:0 0 14px; color:#111827;">EcoPro Drops to KRW 129,200: Why a Stock With Improving Earnings Still Fell Hard on May 15</h1>

    <strong style="font-size:18px;">Closing Snapshot</strong>
    <p style="margin:10px 0 0;">EcoPro closed at KRW 129,200 on May 15, down 9.21% from the previous session. The KOSDAQ itself fell 5.14% to 1,129.82, and EcoPro’s intraday range was unusually wide at KRW 126,100 to KRW 144,800. That matters because the move looked less like a fresh company-specific collapse and more like a broad risk-off shock hitting high-beta KOSDAQ growth names especially hard. 1) 2) 3)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Why the Stock Moved</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The first thing to understand is that May 15 was a market-led day. When the KOSDAQ falls more than 5% in one session, even stocks with improving fundamentals can be dragged lower. EcoPro tends to move more sharply than the broader market because it sits at the intersection of retail-driven trading, battery-materials sentiment and growth-stock positioning. In rising markets, that gives it upside torque. In falling markets, it amplifies the downside. 1) 2) 3)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">For beginners, this is one of the hardest lessons in the market: a company’s recent earnings improvement does not guarantee short-term price stability. Share prices also reflect positioning, liquidity and what investors decide to sell first when fear rises. On Friday, EcoPro looked like one of those positions investors were quick to reduce, not because its recent results had suddenly been disproven, but because the whole KOSDAQ tape turned defensive. This is an inference from the market-wide selloff and EcoPro’s role as a volatile sector proxy. 1) 2) 3) 4)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What the Recent Fundamentals Show</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">The company’s most recent operating message was actually more constructive than the stock action suggested. On April 29, EcoPro said first-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue came in at KRW 822.0 billion and operating profit at KRW 60.2 billion. Revenue rose only about 2% year on year, but operating profit increased roughly 42-fold from KRW 1.4 billion a year earlier. That means EcoPro’s current story is less about explosive top-line growth and more about a meaningful recovery in profitability. 4)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">EcoPro itself highlighted three drivers behind that improvement: the consolidation effect from the Indonesian GEN smelter, rising metal prices and recovery in downstream industries. The company said average lithium hydroxide prices rose from USD 10.3 per kilogram in the fourth quarter of 2025 to USD 18.5 in the first quarter of 2026, helping improve product pricing. That matters because battery-material makers are highly sensitive to raw-material pricing cycles and lagged selling-price adjustments. In plain language, part of the business is finally getting paid better for what it sells. 4)</p>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Subsidiary performance also reinforced that recovery narrative. EcoPro BM posted KRW 605.4 billion in revenue and KRW 20.9 billion in operating profit, while EcoPro Materials delivered KRW 166.5 billion in revenue and KRW 15.7 billion in operating profit. The company pointed to stronger European EV cathode demand, growing ESS-related sales and ongoing support from semiconductor-linked businesses inside the group. So while the market sold the stock hard on Friday, the operating picture across the group still looked considerably healthier than it did a year ago. 4)</p>

  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">Sector and Market Context</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">That is why Friday’s move should be read carefully. The selloff does not automatically mean the battery-material cycle has rolled over again. EcoPro said it expects a stronger pace of earnings improvement from the second quarter as higher mineral prices are more fully reflected in product pricing. It also pointed to the upcoming ramp of its Hungary cathode plant and the continued expansion of Indonesian projects. Those are not proof that the stock must rebound soon, but they are reasons to separate near-term market panic from the company’s medium-term operating direction. 2) 3) 4)</p>
  <h2 style="font-size:23px; margin:26px 0 10px; color:#111827;">What to Watch Next</h2>
  <p style="margin:0 0 14px;">Three checkpoints matter from here. First, whether overall KOSDAQ volatility cools down. Second, whether lithium and nickel pricing continues to support second-quarter product pricing. Third, whether Hungary production and customer diversification in ESS and other end markets start showing up more clearly in reported numbers. EcoPro is now caught between two stories at once: a stock hit hard in a market washout, and a company still trying to prove a real earnings recovery. The next few months will decide which story dominates. 1) 3) 4)</p>
